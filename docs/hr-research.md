# HR research

Independent research on HR and payroll that we can use to back up our pitch, whatever the idea turns out to be. SD Worx's own studies (HR and Payroll Pulse 2026, Belgian surveys) and EU regulation are in [sd-worx-briefing.md](sd-worx-briefing.md), sections 4 and 5. This file adds figures from other sources.

Figures were checked at the source on 29 September 2026, unless the row says otherwise. Sources are listed at the bottom as [R1] to [R29].

## 0. Read this first

- Always say who measured a figure and in which year. Several studies are US only. Say so on the slide, or use them only to show a pattern.
- Six figures that work on almost any HR or payroll slide:
  1. Only 11% of Belgian employees are engaged at work, and 43% felt a lot of stress the previous day (Gallup, 2025). [R3]
  2. Belgium had the second highest job vacancy rate in the EU at the end of 2025: 3.5%, against 2.0% for the EU. [R4]
  3. Long-term absence for depression and burnout rose 32% between 2021 and 2025. Benefits for it cost more than EUR 2.5 billion in 2024. [R6]
  4. Administrative burden cost Belgian enterprises EUR 5.03 billion in 2022, 32% more than in 2020. [R11]
  5. 88% of HR leaders say AI tools have not yet brought significant business value (Gartner, 2025). [R15]
  6. Belgium taxes labour more than any other OECD country: an average single worker keeps 60.5% of gross pay (OECD average 74.9%). [R25]
- For any AI idea, the human review argument is in section 7. It backs the review requirement in [ideas.md](ideas.md).

## 1. Engagement and wellbeing

| Scope   | Finding                                                                                                                                                                        | Source |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| World   | 20% of employees engaged in 2025, the lowest since 2020. Low engagement costs an estimated USD 10 trillion, 9% of global GDP. Manager engagement fell from 31% in 2022 to 22%. | [R1]   |
| Europe  | 12% engaged (the lowest region), 49% thriving, 39% daily stress.                                                                                                               | [R2]   |
| Belgium | 11% engaged, 60% thriving, 43% daily stress, 12% daily anger. 66% say it is a good time to find a job (Europe 57%).                                                            | [R3]   |

Use: Belgian employees are satisfied with their lives but not engaged at work, they are more stressed than the European average and they think finding another job is easy. That makes retention a real risk.

## 2. Belgian labour market

| Topic           | Finding                                                                                                                             | Source |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Vacancies       | Job vacancy rate in Q4 2025: Netherlands 3.9%, Belgium 3.5%, Malta 3.3%. EU 2.0%, euro area 2.2%.                                   | [R4]   |
| Employment rate | 72.8% of people aged 20 to 64 work (Q2 2026). The government target is 80% by 2029. Flanders 77.3%, Wallonia 67.6%, Brussels 64.7%. | [R5]   |
| Older workers   | 60.2% of people aged 55 to 64 work, a share that has been falling for several quarters. ILO unemployment is 6.1%.                   | [R5]   |

Use: Belgium cannot hire its way out of its shortages. Hours saved on HR and payroll admin matter, and keeping people (or getting them back after long absence) matters more than recruiting.

## 4. Payroll

### Belgian payroll landscape

| Topic               | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Source |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Tax on labour       | OECD Taxing Wages 2026: Belgium has the highest tax wedge of all 38 OECD countries, 52.5% for an average single worker in 2025 (OECD average 35.1%). Income tax and employer social contributions make up 79% of it.              | [R25]  |
| Take-home pay       | An average single worker in Belgium keeps 60.5% of gross pay after tax and benefits, the lowest share in the OECD (average 74.9%). For a married worker with two children it is 80.3%, so family situation changes net pay a lot. | [R25]  |
| Sector rules        | On 1 January 2026 Belgium had 98 joint committees (paritaire comités) and 66 sub-committees. Each sets its own minimum wages, indexation, bonuses and premiums.                                                                   | [R26]  |
| Social secretariats | 74% of private sector employees (2,890,054) have their pay calculated by a recognised social secretariat, and 87% of private sector employers (202,897) use one. Not opened at the source.                                        | [R27]  |

Use: Belgian payroll is expensive and hard. The gap between employer cost and net pay is the largest in the OECD, and the rules depend on one of 164 committees plus family situation. That explains why employers outsource and why employees struggle to read their payslip. Since most Belgian payslips go through a social secretariat, an improvement at SD Worx reaches a large part of the workforce.

### Payroll operations in Europe

| Study                                                                               | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Source |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| Forvis Mazars, 2025 (1,000+ payroll, HR and finance leaders in 13 European markets) | Nearly one in three organisations report payroll calculation errors, and one in five say this damaged employee confidence. 60% struggle to keep up with changing labour laws. 62% name frequent regulatory updates as a top operational challenge. 53% rely on external payroll providers. Fewer than one in five use automated monitoring. About 70% of business-critical data passes through payroll. 51% would switch provider for stronger compliance support. | [R28]  |
| PayrollOrg, 2024 (600+ global payroll professionals)                                | 63% name compliance as their biggest challenge, ahead of managing multiple vendors (30%). 41% do not track payroll performance against objectives.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | [R29]  |

Use: payroll teams' main problem is keeping up with rule changes, not calculating. Errors are common even in Europe, and few teams have automated checks. A tool that flags what a new rule changes, or checks a payroll run before it goes out, fits this gap. Compliance support is a reason to switch provider, which matters to SD Worx.

### Payroll accuracy and understanding pay

| Study                                                                | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Source |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| EY, 2022 (US, 508 respondents at companies with 250 to 10,000 staff) | One in five payrolls contains errors. An error costs USD 291 on average to fix. A company of 1,000 employees spends 29 workweeks a year fixing the most common errors. Time and attendance errors alone cost USD 250,000 per 1,000 employees. | [R8]   |
| HiBob, 2025 (US, 2,000 employees)                                    | 44% have had a payroll mistake. 21% lost trust in their employer because of payroll issues. 53% would consider leaving after repeated mistakes. 64% had stress or disruption because of an error.                                             | [R9]   |
| PayFit, 2025 (1,950 workers in France, Spain and the UK)             | Share who fully understand their pay: UK 73%, Spain 54%, France 29%. Share who ask HR or payroll to explain their payslip: France 75%, Spain 64%, UK 47%.                                                                                     | [R10]  |

Use: payroll mistakes cost money and trust, and people who do not understand their payslip ask HR. Belgium was not in the PayFit survey. France, whose payslip is similarly complex, has the lowest understanding (our inference, not a measured Belgian figure).

## 5. Admin burden and complexity

| Topic                | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Source |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Belgian admin burden | Federal Planning Bureau: administrative burden cost enterprises EUR 5.03 billion in 2022 (+32% on 2020) and the self-employed EUR 0.96 billion. Together 1.08% of GDP. Costs fell 20% for large enterprises and rose for small and medium ones. Tax rules cause the largest share, employment rules the second largest. Employment rules explain 40% of the increase between 2020 and 2022. | [R11]  |
| Complexity ranking   | TMF Group Global Business Complexity Index 2023: Belgium is the 13th most complex of 78 jurisdictions. "Payroll processing is very complex" and "fiscal laws are changing annually".                                                                                                                                                                                                        | [R12]  |
| Complexity trend     | GBCI 2026 (81 jurisdictions): employment complexity is driven by new legislation, pay transparency requirements and regional differences in labour rules.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | [R13]  |
| HR admin time        | Eagle Hill, 2026 (US, 200 HR professionals): 51% spend at least half their week on routine admin. 78% regularly enter the same information into several systems. 91% need two or more systems for one HR task. 68% rely on workarounds such as spreadsheets.                                                                                                                                | [R14]  |

Use: admin weighs most on small and medium firms, and employment rules are a big part of it. Retyping the same data into several systems is the concrete pain to show in a demo.

## 6. AI in HR: adoption, value and trust

| Study                               | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                         | Source |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Gartner, July 2025 (114 HR leaders) | 88% say their organisation has not realised significant business value from AI tools. Not opened at the source (Gartner blocks automated access).                                               | [R15]  |
| Gartner, 2024                       | HR's top generative AI priorities: employee-facing chatbot 43%, HR admin, policies and document generation 42%, recruiting 41%. Not opened at the source.                                       | [R16]  |
| Pew, 2023 (US, 11,004 adults)       | 71% oppose AI making the final hiring decision (7% in favour). 66% would not apply to an employer that uses AI in hiring. 81% think workers monitored by AI would feel inappropriately watched. | [R17]  |

Use: AI adoption in HR is no longer the problem, getting value out of it is. Show a measurable outcome (minutes saved, errors caught) instead of "we added AI". People accept AI that helps them, not AI that decides about them. SD Worx's own figure fits here: 64% of employees want AI systems to explain their decisions (briefing section 4).

## 7. Human review and automation bias

| Rule or study                                             | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Source |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| EU AI Act, Article 14(4)                                  | For high-risk systems, the people overseeing the AI must "remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output" (automation bias) and be able to "disregard, override or reverse the output".   | [R18]  |
| Beck, Eckman, Kern and Kreuter, 2025 (2,784 participants) | When fixing a flagged AI error required typing a correction, people made fewer corrections and accepted more wrong suggestions. Attitude towards AI was the strongest predictor of accuracy: people sceptical of AI caught more errors. | [R19]  |

What this means for our review tool (our inference from the two sources):

- Make correcting as cheap as accepting. One click to choose the right value, not a form to retype it.
- Show the source next to each extracted value, so checking takes seconds.
- Put low-confidence items first and never pre-approve anything.
- Log who approved what, so the jury sees the audit trail.

## 10. Pay equity

| Topic   | Finding                                                                                                                             | Source |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| EU      | Eurostat: women earned 11.1% less per hour than men in the EU in 2024 (unadjusted). Highest Estonia 18.8%, lowest Luxembourg -0.8%. | [R23]  |

## Known gaps

- Gartner and WEF block automated access. Their figures come from press coverage and their own press release titles. Open them in a browser before they go on a slide.
- EY, HiBob, Eagle Hill and Pew are US studies. PayFit covers France, Spain and the UK. We found no Belgian study on payroll errors or payslip understanding.
- The EY and HiBob figures are published by payroll software vendors (Paycom and HiBob).
- Belgium's rank in the GBCI 2025 and 2026 was not found (13th in 2023).
- The social secretariat figures [R27] come from the USS (union of social secretariats) as quoted in search results. Its figures page did not show them and no year is given. Confirm before using.
- The Forvis Mazars press release does not list the 13 markets, so we do not know whether Belgium is included.
