# Challenge briefing

The SD Worx challenge as presented at the preselection on 30 September 2026 by David Smets, product owner of My SD Worx. The summary comes first, the lightly cleaned transcript follows.

## Summary

**The challenge:** build a proof of concept that turns fragmented organisational knowledge into a trusted, shared resource. It must make it easier to **find**, **trust** and **share** information and knowledge.

**Explicitly not wanted:**

- A SharePoint with a search function.
- Another AI agent. SD Worx already has both and still has the problem.

**Advice from the brief:** pick one focus problem and solve that. Do not try to solve everything the theme touches. The guiding questions on the slide are guidance, not a checklist.

**Root cause as SD Worx sees it:** knowledge is spread over documents, manuals, checklists, SharePoint sites, Teams channels and chats, old emails and people's heads (undocumented). Combined with fast growth, the company has "turned into a maze". Behind every product name there is a team that holds knowledge about it.

**The two examples from the brief:**

1. **Onboarding to a customer (Nike).** In 2010 a new account owner got one Word document and a 30 minute call, and knew everything about the customer. Today Nike uses many SD Worx tools in several European countries with many teams. A new account owner would not know who to call or which document to trust.
2. **Urgent employee question on the phone.** A service colleague asks the internal agent for guidance and gets three documents: one with no owner, one edited last week (so probably current?), one with the right title but from another country. A colleague then emails a fourth document. Four sources, no way to tell which is right, and the customer is waiting.

The recurring trust signals in these examples: **owner** (who wrote it, who to ask), **freshness** (when it was last checked, not just edited), **scope** (which country, customer or product it applies to) and **people** (who knows this).
